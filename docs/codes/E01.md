## What happened

The thermocouple on the motor winding reached `TRIP_THRESHOLD` — 110 °C. The
supervisor opened its relay, which broke the contactor coil circuit and dropped
the motor. This is the trip the whole system exists to perform.

Nothing is broken. The motor got too hot and was stopped before it cooked.

## Before you touch anything

The fix below means putting your hands near the motor fan. **Lock out the machine
disconnect first** — physically lock the breaker off, do not just trust that the
saw is stopped. The supervisor re-closes its relay on its own once the motor
cools, and from that moment a press of the Start button spins the fan.

## Do this now

1. Leave the saw alone until the LED stops flashing fast and goes to a slow
   blink. That is the motor cooling below `RESET_THRESHOLD` (70 °C).
2. Lock out the disconnect, then look into the fan shroud on the back of the
   motor — the cover over the fan at the non-drive end.
3. If it is packed with sawdust, and it usually is, blow it out with compressed
   air until you can see the fan blades and the cooling fins clearly.
4. Check the dust extraction that was supposed to keep it clean.
5. Restore the disconnect. Wait for the LED to go solid, then press Start.
6. Watch the first cut. If the LED goes to fast blink again within a few minutes
   of normal cutting, stop and read [If it keeps happening](#if-it-keeps-happening).

## Why it happens

This motor is a TEFC — Totally Enclosed Fan-Cooled. It is sealed against dust,
which means it cannot shed heat through ventilation. Every watt it loses goes out
through the finned frame, and the only thing moving air over those fins is the
shaft-driven fan behind the shroud.

Pack that shroud with sawdust and the motor has no cooling at all. It will then
overheat at completely normal current, doing completely normal work.

It also has a **service factor of 1.0**, meaning zero designed thermal margin.
Motors rated 1.15 can be pushed; this one cannot. Rated load is the limit.

**A blocked shroud is the cause of most trips on this machine.** It is what
destroyed the original Klixon protector — it tripped correctly, over and over,
for years, until the mechanism wore out. Other possible causes, in rough order:

- A genuinely heavy cut — thick hardwood, a dull blade, a slow feed rate.
- A dull or pitchy blade making the motor work far harder than the cut suggests.
- High shop ambient temperature stacking on top of normal operating heat.
- A belt tensioned too tight, adding mechanical drag.

Note what is *not* on this list: an electrical fault. The starter's overload
heaters watch current. This code is about temperature, and the two are not the
same thing — the original failure had the motor drawing perfectly normal current
the entire time it was overheating.

## When it clears

Automatically, in stages:

1. Winding falls below `RESET_THRESHOLD` (70 °C) → the LED changes to a slow
   blink. The saw still will not start.
2. It stays below 70 °C for `COOLDOWN_HOLD` (120 seconds) → the LED goes solid
   and the relay re-closes.
3. **You press Start.**

The saw does not restart on its own, ever. The relay closing only makes starting
*possible* again. The starter's 3-wire seal-in circuit requires a physical press
of the Start button, which is a property of the wiring, not of the firmware.

From a hard trip, expect several minutes. A 3 HP TEFC frame with a blocked
shroud cools slowly — that is the same problem that caused the trip.

## If it keeps happening

Three trips within ten minutes puts the supervisor into `MANUAL_LOCKOUT`. See
[E04](E04.md).

If you have cleaned the shroud and it still trips under normal cutting:

- **Check the blade.** A dull or resin-loaded blade is the most commonly missed
  cause. It loads the motor far past what the cut appears to demand.
- **Confirm the shroud is actually clear**, not just clear at the opening. Dust
  packs behind the fan where you cannot see it without removing the cover.
- **Check the overload heaters are correctly sized.** With 14.4 A full-load
  current and service factor 1.0, the NEC ceiling is 16.5 A. Oversized heaters
  from a previous owner mean the current-protection half of this machine has
  been compromised the whole time.
- **Stop using the saw** if it trips on light cuts with a clean shroud and a
  sharp blade. That points at the motor itself — a bearing dragging, or winding
  damage from the years it spent overheating before this system existed. Get the
  builder.

Do not raise `TRIP_THRESHOLD` to make this stop. Class F insulation is rated to
155 °C for the total system, the passive thermostat opens at 120–130 °C, and this
motor has already been thermally abused for years. The 110 °C threshold is
deliberately conservative and it is the last cheap warning before winding damage.
