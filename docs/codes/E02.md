## What happened

The supervisor read the thermocouple on the motor winding and rejected the
answer. One of five things was true: the MAX31855 amplifier flagged an open
circuit, a short to ground, or a short to VCC; the value landed outside the
−20 to 400 °C band the firmware will accept; or the reading jumped more than
50 °C since the last good one.

It opened the relay, which broke the contactor coil circuit and dropped the
motor.

**The motor may be stone cold.** This code is about the measuring chain — probe,
leads, terminals — not about heat.

## Before you touch anything

The thermocouple leads run into the same enclosure as the contactor, and their
far end is bonded to winding end turns sitting at 230 V. Lock the machine
disconnect OFF and tag it before you open anything. Verify the coil-circuit
terminals read 0 V with a meter you have just tested on a known-live circuit.
Safety glasses.

## Do this now

1. Check the LED is a **double-blink then pause**. A fast blink is a real
   overtemperature and a different problem — see [E01](E01.md).
2. Lock out the disconnect as above.
3. Open the enclosure and find the MAX31855 board. Reseat both thermocouple
   screw terminals. Confirm each lead is captured under its screw — yellow on +,
   red on − — and not just resting against it.
4. Follow the K-type leads from the enclosure back to the motor. Look for a lead
   pulled taut, pinched under a cover, or rubbed through where it crosses the
   frame.
5. Restore the disconnect. Wait for the LED to go solid, then press Start.
6. If it double-blinks again, stop and read
   [If it keeps happening](#if-it-keeps-happening).

## Why it happens

`SR-5` makes any sensor doubt a trip. There is deliberately no "last known good"
value and no "assume ambient" fallback — a protection system that guesses the
winding temperature is not protection. So a bad reading stops the saw exactly
the way a hot motor does.

The causes are physical, and this machine is hard on wiring. The motor is
belt-driven and cabinet-mounted, so everything in there shakes, and the shop is
full of sawdust. In rough order of likelihood:

- **A lead chafed against the motor frame or an enclosure edge.** Insulation
  wears through, the conductor touches metal, and the MAX31855 reports a short
  to ground.
- **A connector or screw terminal shaken loose.** Vibration plus heat cycling
  backs terminals out. A lead that is half out reads as an open circuit,
  usually intermittently.
- **The K-type junction itself failing.** It is a thin dissimilar-metal weld
  bonded to end turns that heat and cool every session. It is the most
  stressed part of the chain.
- **SPI leads picking up noise from the motor.** The run from the MAX31855 to
  the ESP32 is low-level digital signalling a few inches from a 14.4 A motor
  circuit. A long or unshielded run gives unstable readings, which show up here
  as implausible jumps.
- **Swapped polarity, if anyone has recently had the leads apart.** On K-type,
  yellow is + and red is −. Inverted, the reading runs backwards — cold reads
  high, hot reads low — and usually trips the range or jump check as soon as the
  motor starts changing temperature.

What is *not* on this list is the motor. Nothing here says the winding is hot.

## When it clears

Automatically, once the supervisor is getting readings it trusts again.

Nothing clears while the fault is present. The loop rejects the reading, trips,
and starts over. Once valid readings return:

1. A good reading below `RESET_THRESHOLD` (70 °C) → the LED changes to a slow
   blink.
2. It stays below 70 °C for `COOLDOWN_HOLD` (120 seconds) → the LED goes solid
   and the relay re-closes.
3. **You press Start.**

If the motor was cold the whole time — which is the usual case for this code —
that is about two minutes of slow blink and nothing else.

The saw does not restart on its own, ever. Re-closing the relay only makes
starting *possible* again. The starter is a 3-wire momentary-Start /
maintained-Stop circuit with a seal-in auxiliary contact: once the coil circuit
is broken the contactor drops and stays dropped until a human presses the
physical Start button. That is a property of the wiring (`SR-4`), not of the
firmware.

If the fault does not clear, expect the LED to move on to a triple-blink. Three
trips inside `TRIP_WINDOW_MIN` (10 minutes) put the supervisor into
`MANUAL_LOCKOUT` so it stops cycling the relay open and closed. That is
[E04](E04.md), and it is not a second failure — it is this one, repeating.

## If it keeps happening

An intermittent sensor fault is still a real fault. Do not run the saw between
episodes hoping it settles.

- **Dress and secure the leads.** They must be strapped so they cannot chafe
  against the winding or the frame, and routed clear of the rotor. Hand-turn the
  shaft through a full revolution to confirm clearance if you have had the motor
  open.
- **Shorten and tidy the SPI run** — GPIO18 (SCK), GPIO19 (DO), GPIO5 (CS), 3V3
  and GND — and keep it away from motor conductors.
- **If anyone has re-terminated the probe recently**, verify polarity (yellow +,
  red −) and re-run the two-point check: ice bath should read 0.0 ± 1 °C,
  boiling water 100.0 ± 2 °C. Cold high and hot low means the junction is
  inverted.
- **If the supervisor gets no answer at all** rather than an answer it dislikes,
  that is a different code and different hardware — see [E03](E03.md).
- **Get the builder** if the fault follows the probe rather than the wiring.
  Replacing the thermocouple at the winding means pulling the motor's end bell
  and re-bonding to the AlN substrate. That is not an operator job.

Do not unplug the ESP32 to make this stop. It would not help anyway — the relay
is energized-to-close, so an unpowered supervisor leaves the coil circuit open
and the saw will not start at all.
