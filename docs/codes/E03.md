## What happened

The protection loop expects a fresh winding temperature four times a second
(`SAMPLE_HZ`, 4 Hz). Nothing valid arrived for `SENSOR_TIMEOUT` — 3 seconds,
3000 ms. The supervisor opened its relay, broke the contactor coil circuit, and
dropped the motor.

This is not the same as [E02](E02.md), and the difference tells you where to
look:

- **E02** — the supervisor got an answer and did not believe it. That points at
  the **thermocouple**: the probe, its leads, its terminals.
- **E03** — the supervisor got no answer at all. That points at the **link
  between the ESP32 and the MAX31855**: the SPI bus, the amplifier board itself,
  or its 3V3 supply.

A broken thermocouple does not land here. The amplifier still answers, and the
answer carries an open-circuit flag — that is E02.

## Before you touch anything

Everything this code points at — the MAX31855 board, its jumpers, its 3V3 feed —
lives in the same enclosure as the contactor, inches from 240 V. Lock the machine
disconnect OFF and tag it before you open the cover. Verify the coil-circuit
terminals read 0 V with a meter you have just tested on a known-live circuit.
Safety glasses.

## Do this now

1. Note that the LED shows the same **double-blink then pause** for both sensor
   codes. The event log on the dashboard is what tells E02 and E03 apart.
2. Lock out the disconnect as above.
3. Open the enclosure. Check the five wires between the ESP32 and the MAX31855:
   GPIO18 to SCK, GPIO19 to DO, GPIO5 to CS, 3V3 to VIN, GND to GND. Push each
   one home. Look for a jumper backed half out of its header.
4. Check the amplifier board is seated square and that nothing has been snagged
   or pinched by the cover.
5. Restore the disconnect. Wait for the LED to go solid, then press Start.
6. If it double-blinks again, stop and read
   [If it keeps happening](#if-it-keeps-happening).

## Why it happens

`SR-5` treats a stale reading exactly like a bad one. There is no "carry the
last value forward" path — if the supervisor cannot see the winding right now,
the saw stops.

Most likely first:

- **A jumper backed out of a header.** This is a belt-driven, cabinet-mounted
  saw; the enclosure shakes every time the motor starts. A single loose lead on
  SCK, DO, or CS silences the whole bus.
- **The amplifier lost its 3V3 supply.** A lifted wire or a cold joint on the
  power pin. No power, no answer, and the temperature line simply goes quiet.
- **The MAX31855 board itself has failed**, or a header joint on it has cracked.
- **Noise coupling from the motor circuit.** A long or unshielded SPI run beside
  a 14.4 A motor conductor usually shows up as unstable *readings* — that is
  E02 — but a badly corrupted bus can leave the loop with nothing it can use.

Note what this is not. It is not the motor and it is not heat. It is also not a
dead supervisor: if the whole ESP32 had stopped, the LED would be off or on the
SOS pattern and the relay would be open for that reason instead — see
[E05](E05.md).

## When it clears

Automatically, once readings start arriving again:

1. A good reading below `RESET_THRESHOLD` (70 °C) → the LED changes to a slow
   blink.
2. It stays below 70 °C for `COOLDOWN_HOLD` (120 seconds) → the LED goes solid
   and the relay re-closes.
3. **You press Start.**

The motor is almost certainly already cold for this code, so what you are
waiting on is the 120-second hold, not cooling.

The saw does not restart on its own, ever. Re-closing the relay only makes
starting *possible* again. The starter is a 3-wire momentary-Start /
maintained-Stop circuit with a seal-in auxiliary contact: break the coil circuit
and the contactor drops and stays dropped until someone presses the physical
Start button. That is a property of the wiring (`SR-4`), not of the firmware.

If the bus stays quiet, the supervisor will count three trips inside
`TRIP_WINDOW_MIN` (10 minutes) and drop into `MANUAL_LOCKOUT` rather than keep
cycling the relay. That is [E04](E04.md).

## If it keeps happening

**Firmware note, worth knowing before you chase hardware.** The reference
pseudocode in ARCHITECTURE.md routes the staleness check through the same
`trip("SENSOR_FAULT")` call as the range, fault-flag, and implausible-jump
checks, so as written the firmware cannot distinguish this code from E02 — the
timeout branch must emit its own distinct cause for E03 to appear in the log at
all.

Otherwise:

- **Intermittent dropouts that only happen under load** point at vibration or
  electrical noise, not at the probe. Re-dress the SPI run short, strapped down,
  and away from motor conductors.
- **Re-run the bench check.** Disconnecting the sensor must open the relay
  every time. If it does not, stop using the saw and get the builder — that is a
  failure of the protection itself, not of the sensor.
- **Get the builder** to swap the MAX31855 or rebuild the harness. Replacing an
  amplifier board and re-terminating a five-wire bus inside a live starter
  enclosure is builder work.

Do not unplug the ESP32 to clear the double-blink. The relay is
energized-to-close — an unpowered supervisor holds the coil circuit open and the
saw will not start at all.
