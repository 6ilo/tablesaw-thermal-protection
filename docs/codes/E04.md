## What happened

`MAX_CONSECUTIVE_TRIPS` — 3 — trips occurred inside a rolling
`TRIP_WINDOW_MIN` (10) minute window. On the third one the supervisor stopped
recovering by itself. It went into `MANUAL_LOCKOUT`, left its relay open, and
changed the LED to a triple-blink then long pause.

The saw will not start, and waiting will not change that. This state clears only
when a person presses the ack button.

Nothing new broke on trip three. The trips themselves are the fault — almost
always [E01](E01.md), sometimes [E02](E02.md).

## Do this now

1. **Do not press ack yet.** Getting here fixed nothing, and clearing it fixes
   nothing either.
2. Open the dashboard trip log and read the last three entries. `OVERTEMP` means
   the motor cooked — go to [E01](E01.md). `SENSOR_FAULT` means the measuring
   chain is bad — go to [E02](E02.md).
3. Fix that cause. For overtemperature that is the fan shroud, and [E01](E01.md)
   has the procedure including the lockout it requires.
4. Check the winding temperature on the dashboard. Wait until it is below
   `RESET_THRESHOLD` (70 °C).
5. Press the ack button once — the local ack button wired to GPIO27, inside the
   enclosure alongside the starter. If reaching it means opening that
   enclosure, lock out the disconnect first.
6. LED goes to a slow blink, then solid after `COOLDOWN_HOLD` (120 seconds).
   Press Start.

## Why it happens

A single trip is the system working. `TRIPPED` → `COOLDOWN` → `ARMED` is an
automatic sequence, and it is meant to be — the saw stops, the motor cools, the
relay re-closes, you press Start and carry on.

Three of those inside ten minutes is a different message. It means the condition
never actually went away. The supervisor is not recovering, it is looping, and a
system that silently cycles a relay open and closed every few minutes is hiding a
real problem behind a lot of log noise. So it stops and asks for a human. That is
the whole purpose of this state.

Most likely, in rough order:

- **The fan shroud is still blocked.** The motor hits `TRIP_THRESHOLD` (110 °C),
  falls to 70 °C sitting in the cabinet, gets started again, and is straight back
  over 110 °C inside a cut or two. A TEFC — Totally Enclosed Fan-Cooled — motor
  sheds heat only through air moving over its finned frame, and with a service
  factor of 1.0 there is no margin to absorb the shortfall. This is the failure
  the whole retrofit was built around.
- **An intermittent sensor fault.** A chafed or half-seated thermocouple lead
  makes and breaks contact as the machine vibrates. That trips, clears, and trips
  again far faster than heat ever could — three inside ten minutes is easy. See
  [E02](E02.md).
- **A dull or resin-loaded blade** loading the motor hard on every cut, so it
  reheats to 110 °C almost as fast as it cooled.
- **Someone pressing Start the moment the LED goes solid**, three times running,
  without looking at why it tripped the first time.

The counter is a rolling window, not a lifetime total. Three trips spread across
an afternoon do not lock the saw out; three inside ten minutes do.

## When it clears

Only on a physical press of the ack button, and only with the winding below
`RESET_THRESHOLD` (70 °C). Nothing else clears it:

- Not temperature. The motor can go stone cold and the lockout stays.
- Not time. There is no timer running.
- Not a power cycle. The lockout is written to the ESP32's flash when it is
  entered, so a reboot comes straight back into it — that is [E07](E07.md).
- A press made while the winding is still at or above 70 °C is rejected and
  logged. The LED keeps triple-blinking and nothing happens. That is
  [E08](E08.md), and it is the expected result of being impatient.

On a good press the supervisor moves to `COOLDOWN`, resets the trip counter to
zero, and runs the normal `COOLDOWN_HOLD` (120 seconds) before the LED goes
solid and the relay re-closes.

**Then you press Start.** The saw does not restart on its own, ever. Re-closing
the relay only makes starting *possible* again. The starter is a 3-wire
momentary-Start / maintained-Stop circuit with a seal-in auxiliary contact — once
the coil circuit is broken the contactor drops and stays dropped until a human
presses the physical Start button. That is a property of the wiring (`SR-4`), not
of the firmware.

## If it keeps happening

Clearing a lockout is an acknowledgement, not a repair. If you clear it and the
saw locks out again in the same session, the cause was never dealt with, and
pressing ack a second time just tells the supervisor to stop mentioning it.

- **Go back to the trip log and read the cause**, not the lockout. The lockout is
  a symptom of three of those.
- **Overtemperature trips → [E01](E01.md).** Clear the shroud properly, behind
  the fan where you cannot see it from the opening. Check the blade. Check the
  overload heaters are sized to the 16.5 A ceiling that 14.4 A full-load current
  and service factor 1.0 imply.
- **Sensor faults → [E02](E02.md).** Dress and secure the leads, reseat the
  terminals at the MAX31855.
- **Get the builder** if the trip log shows no consistent cause, if the causes
  are mixed, or if you find yourself clearing lockouts more than once in a
  working day. At that point the machine has told you three separate times that
  something is wrong, and the answer is not another button press.

Do not treat the ack button as part of a start procedure. It has exactly one
power — removing the "we have seen enough trips to distrust the automation" flag.
It cannot lower `TRIP_THRESHOLD`, cannot close the relay, and cannot override an
active fault. That is deliberate.
