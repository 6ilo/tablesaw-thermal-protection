## What happened

At every power-up the supervisor takes 10 thermocouple readings, 100 ms apart,
and requires at least 8 of them to come back good before it will arm. This time
it got fewer than 8 — possibly none at all.

It stopped there. It logged `BOOT_SENSOR_FAIL`, left the relay open, and put the
LED on the SOS pattern. The protection loop never started. The saw cannot be
started.

**Nothing was ever at risk.** The relay is energized-to-close and it was never
closed — the coil circuit has been open since power-up. The supervisor declined
to protect a saw whose temperature it cannot measure. That is the system working
correctly, not failing.

## Before you touch anything

The first step below is a power cycle at the disconnect and needs no tools.
Everything past that — probe leads, terminals, the MAX31855 — is inside the
enclosure with the contactor at 240 V. Lock the machine disconnect OFF and tag
it before you open the cover, and verify the coil-circuit terminals read 0 V
with a meter you have just tested on a known-live circuit.

## Do this now

1. Do not use the saw. SOS means no temperature protection is running.
2. Power-cycle at the machine disconnect: off, count to ten, on. The disconnect
   de-energizes the supervisor, so this genuinely restarts it.
3. Watch the LED. A good boot goes to a slow blink for `COOLDOWN_HOLD`
   (120 seconds), then solid. Solid means the self-test passed — press Start and
   carry on.
4. If it returns to SOS, lock out the disconnect and check the thermocouple at
   both ends: yellow on + and red on − under the MAX31855 screw terminals, and
   the lead run down to the motor for a pulled, pinched, or chafed conductor.
5. Restore power. Still SOS — stop, and read
   [If it keeps happening](#if-it-keeps-happening).

## Why it happens

The self-test counts only readings that come back clean. The reasons one does
not are the same physical faults that produce [E02](E02.md) and [E03](E03.md)
while running — the difference is timing. Here the measuring chain was already
broken at power-up, so the supervisor never armed at all.

Most likely first:

- **A thermocouple lead disconnected or broken**, so the MAX31855 reports an
  open circuit on every read. This is the usual answer if anyone has been
  working on the machine.
- **A lead chafed through against the motor frame** and shorting. The motor is
  belt-driven and cabinet-mounted; leads that are not strapped down move every
  time it runs.
- **No answer from the amplifier at all** — an SPI jumper off its header, or the
  3V3 supply to the MAX31855 missing. Same hardware as [E03](E03.md).
- **Marginal wiring that gives 6 or 7 good reads out of 10.** That is the
  interesting case: the connection is intermittent rather than dead, and it
  would not have survived a cutting session. The self-test caught what a single
  lucky reading would have missed.

This says nothing about the motor's temperature. The supervisor never got far
enough to form an opinion about the winding.

## When it clears

Only by removing and restoring power. That is the whole recovery path.

There is no automatic route out of this state. The boot routine stops before it
starts the protection loop, so nothing re-tests the sensor while the LED is on
SOS — waiting does not help, and the relay stays open the entire time.

After a clean boot the saw still does not start on its own. The relay re-closes
at the end of the 120-second cooldown and a human presses the physical Start
button. The starter is a 3-wire momentary-Start / maintained-Stop circuit with a
seal-in auxiliary contact, so a dropped contactor stays dropped until that
button is pressed. That is a property of the wiring (`SR-4`), not of the
firmware.

## If it keeps happening

Two SOS boots in a row is not an intermittent — the probe wiring is genuinely
broken. Stop and get the builder.

- **Builder task:** meter the thermocouple and the amplifier's 3V3 supply on the
  bench, then re-run commissioning step 1. Disconnecting the sensor must open
  the relay every time.
- **If the probe has to be replaced at the winding**, the motor's end bell comes
  off and the sensor is re-bonded to the AlN substrate with its lead routing and
  rotor clearance re-checked. That is not an operator job.

Do not unplug the ESP32 or jumper across the relay contacts to get cutting
again. Beyond the obvious, it does not work: with the supervisor unpowered the
relay is open by design and the contactor will not pull in.
