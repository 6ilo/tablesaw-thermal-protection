## What happened

The supervisor lost power and started up again. Its boot self-test passed — so
the thermocouple chain is fine — and then it read the state it had saved to flash
and found `MANUAL_LOCKOUT`. It went straight back into lockout, left its relay
open, and logged `BOOT_INTO_LOCKOUT`. The LED is a triple-blink then long pause.

Nothing new went wrong. This is the same lockout you already had, on the other
side of a reboot.

## Do this now

1. Read [E04](E04.md). That is the real code; this one is only how it looks after
   a power cycle. Everything there applies.
2. Deal with whatever tripped the saw three times before you clear anything.
3. Wait until the dashboard shows the winding below `RESET_THRESHOLD` (70 °C).
4. Press the ack button once — the local ack button wired to GPIO27, inside the
   enclosure alongside the starter. Lock out the disconnect first if reaching it
   means opening that enclosure. LED goes slow blink, then solid after
   `COOLDOWN_HOLD` (120 seconds). Press Start.

## Why it happens

Usually because someone cut the power hoping it would reset the saw. That is a
reasonable thing to try and it clears plenty of machines. It does not clear this
one, by design.

`MANUAL_LOCKOUT` is written to the ESP32's flash the moment it is entered, and on
the next boot the persisted lockout **overrides the temperature-based boot
decision entirely**. This is where it differs from [E06](E06.md): a held trip is
conditional — reboot with the motor cool and the supervisor comes up in cooldown.
A lockout is not conditional. Cold motor, cold shop, a week later, still lockout.

The reason is what the lockout means. Temperature was never what the supervisor
was waiting on. It stopped trusting its own automatic recovery after three trips
in ten minutes, and what it is waiting on is a person. A power cycle does not
supply a person.

You can also arrive here without anyone touching the disconnect — a supply blip
in the shop, or the supervisor's own watchdog resetting it. Same result.

## When it clears

Exactly as [E04](E04.md) clears: one physical press of the ack button, with the
winding below `RESET_THRESHOLD` (70 °C). Not on temperature, not on a timer, and
not on another power cycle — that just logs this code again.

A press made while the winding is still at or above 70 °C is rejected and logged
as [E08](E08.md). Wait for the temperature, then press again.

After a good press the supervisor drops to cooldown, zeroes the trip counter,
holds for `COOLDOWN_HOLD` (120 seconds), then goes solid and re-closes the relay.
**Then you press Start.** The saw does not restart on its own. The relay closing
only makes starting possible again — the starter is a 3-wire momentary-Start /
maintained-Stop circuit with a seal-in auxiliary contact, so the contactor stays
dropped until a human presses the physical Start button. That is a property of
the wiring (`SR-4`), not of the firmware.

## If it keeps happening

- **If you are seeing this because you keep power-cycling to escape a lockout**,
  it will keep happening. Press ack instead, once the motor is cool.
- **If you see it several times inside a working day**, the supervisor is losing
  power on its own while the saw runs — a loose tap connection or a supply
  fault. One boot when the disconnect is switched on is expected; the supervisor
  is powered from downstream of it. Repeated unexplained reboots are a builder
  task. The log records every boot.
- **If it appears with no lockout anywhere in the trip log**, the saved state is
  suspect rather than the machine. Get the builder; do not keep rebooting.
