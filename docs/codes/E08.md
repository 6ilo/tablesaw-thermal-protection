## What happened

You pressed the ack button while the winding was still at or above
`RESET_THRESHOLD` (70 °C). The supervisor ignored the press and wrote it to the
log. The saw stays in `MANUAL_LOCKOUT` and the LED keeps its triple-blink then
long pause.

This is not a fault. It is the machine telling you a button press did nothing,
which is otherwise invisible and maddening. You were early, that is all.

## Do this now

1. Wait. Watch the winding temperature on the dashboard.
2. When it reads below 70 °C, press ack again. From a hot trip on a 3 HP TEFC —
   Totally Enclosed Fan-Cooled — motor, expect several minutes.
3. LED goes to a slow blink, then solid after `COOLDOWN_HOLD` (120 seconds).
   Press Start.

## Why it happens

Clearing a lockout hands the saw back to automatic control, so the supervisor
requires the winding to be genuinely cool first — the same 70 °C it requires
before leaving any trip. Below that line the press works. At or above it, the
press is refused.

The usual reason is timing. You cleaned the shroud, you are ready, the motor is
not. It cools slowest in exactly the situation that caused the lockout, because a
TEFC frame sheds heat only through air moving over its fins.

## When it clears

There is nothing to clear. This is a log entry, not a state — the supervisor
never left `MANUAL_LOCKOUT`.

The lockout you were pressing against is [E04](E04.md), and it clears on a press
made below 70 °C.

## If it keeps happening

Repeated rejected presses just mean you are still early. Wait longer between
them; pressing more often does not lower the threshold.

Two things are worth noticing:

- **Holding the button down does not help.** In `MANUAL_LOCKOUT` a long press
  does exactly what a short press does.
- **If the dashboard shows the winding well below 70 °C and presses are still
  rejected**, something disagrees — either the button wiring (GPIO27 to ground)
  or the reading the protection loop trusts. That is a builder task, not an
  operator one.
