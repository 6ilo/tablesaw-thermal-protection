## What happened

The winding passed `WARN_THRESHOLD` — 95 °C. `TRIP_THRESHOLD` is 110 °C. The
supervisor raised an advisory alert and did nothing else. The relay stayed
closed, the contactor stayed in, the saw is still running, and the state is still
`ARMED`.

Every trip and lockout code means the saw has already stopped. This one means it
is heading that way and you still get to choose when to stop — which is the
difference between finishing a cut and being stopped in the middle of one.

**The status LED does not change for this.** It stays solid, the same as any
normal armed motor. The only place this warning appears is the local web
dashboard.

## Before you touch anything

Stopping and waiting needs no lockout. Putting a hand near the fan shroud does.

- Press Stop, then lock the machine disconnect in the OFF position and tag it
  before you open the cabinet or go near the shroud at the back of the motor.
- Assume the frame and the shroud are hot after a 95 °C winding. Let them cool
  before you reach in.
- Compressed air and eye protection only after the disconnect is locked out.

## Do this now

1. Finish the cut you are in, if it is nearly done. Treat the 15 °C of headroom
   as enough to finish a cut and not enough to start one.
2. Do not start another cut, and do not start a heavy rip in thick stock. That is
   the fastest way to turn this warning into [E01](E01.md) with the blade buried
   in the workpiece.
3. Press Stop and let the motor sit. Watch the number on the dashboard until it
   turns around and starts falling.
4. At the next natural break, lock out and check the fan shroud on the back of
   the motor. If it is packed with sawdust, blow it out until you can see the
   fan blades and the cooling fins.
5. Go back to cutting once the reading is near where the session started.

## Why it happens

95 °C is not a fault. It is a working motor losing the race between the heat it
is making and the heat it can get rid of.

This motor is a TEFC — Totally Enclosed Fan-Cooled. It is sealed against
sawdust, so it cannot vent heat. Everything it sheds goes out through the finned
frame, and the only thing moving air over those fins is the shaft-driven fan
behind the shroud. It also has a **service factor of 1.0** — no designed thermal
margin at all. Rated load is the limit.

Most likely causes, in rough order:

- **The fan shroud packing up with sawdust.** This is the documented root cause
  of the original protector failure on this saw, and it is where to look first.
- A long session of heavy cuts back to back, with no idle time between them for
  the fan to catch up.
- A dull or resin-loaded blade, which loads the motor far past what the cut looks
  like it should demand.
- High shop ambient temperature stacking on normal operating heat.
- A belt tensioned too tight, adding mechanical drag.

None of this shows up as an electrical fault. The starter's overload heaters
watch amps, and a motor with a blocked shroud draws perfectly normal current the
whole time it is cooking. Temperature is the only signal that catches it, which
is the entire reason this retrofit exists.

## When it clears

By itself. The supervisor stops raising it as soon as the winding falls back
below 95 °C. There is nothing to press and nothing to reset.

Because the state never left `ARMED`, nothing about starting the saw changed. The
relay never opened, so it does not need to re-close. If you stopped the saw to
let it cool, you press Start again when you are ready — the saw has no way to
start itself. The starter is a 3-wire momentary-Start / maintained-Stop circuit
with a seal-in auxiliary contact, so every start is a human pressing a button.

If the winding keeps climbing to 110 °C instead, the supervisor trips, the
contactor drops, and the saw stops wherever it is. That is [E01](E01.md).

You can acknowledge the alert on the dashboard, or with a short press of the ack
button, to clear the flag off the display. That clears the message only. It does
not change the temperature and it does not move any threshold.

## If it keeps happening

**Keep the dashboard open on your phone during long sessions.** There is no LED
for this warning, so an unwatched dashboard means you find out about the heat
when the saw stops instead.

- **Every session** means the shroud is packing up faster than your dust
  extraction can prevent. That is the root cause, and cleaning it is an operator
  job.
- **Earlier in each session at the same workload** means the motor is heating
  faster than it used to. The dashboard tracks this as a °C/min figure and raises
  `HEATING_FASTER_THAN_BASELINE` when it exceeds 1.5× the baseline recorded on
  the first commissioning run. Treat that as the cleaning being due — it is a
  warning you get *before* a trip, which is the genuinely new capability here.
- **On light cuts, with a clean shroud and a sharp blade,** stop and get the
  builder. That points at the motor itself rather than at cooling.

Raising `WARN_THRESHOLD` (95 °C) to make this stop appearing buys you nothing.
`TRIP_THRESHOLD` (110 °C) does not move with it, the passive thermostat behind it
does not move at all, and you would be deleting the only part of this system that
can save you a stopped cut.
