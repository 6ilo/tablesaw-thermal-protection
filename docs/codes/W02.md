## What happened

The supervisor fits a straight line through the last 60 seconds of winding
temperature samples — about 240 of them, at `SAMPLE_HZ` (4 Hz) — and reads the
slope off it in °C per minute. That slope has come in at more than 1.5 times
`baseline_ror`, the figure recorded during the first commissioning run under load.

Nothing tripped. The relay stayed closed, the state is still `ARMED`, and the saw
is still cutting. The winding need not be anywhere near hot for this to fire — it
can be sitting at 60 °C. `WARN_THRESHOLD` (95 °C) and `TRIP_THRESHOLD` (110 °C)
are about *where* the number is. This alert is about *how fast it is moving*.

This is the one thing the old protection could never do. The Klixon `BEC2921`
opened only once the motor was already too hot, and the starter's overload heaters
watch amps — a motor with a blocked shroud draws normal current the whole time it
is cooking. A climbing °C/min figure at unchanged workload is the signal that
arrives *before* the heat.

**The LED does not change for this.** It stays solid, exactly like any normal
armed motor. The alert appears on the local web dashboard and nowhere else.

## Before you touch anything

Reading a number on your phone needs no lockout. Putting a hand or an air nozzle
behind the motor does.

- Press Stop, then lock the machine disconnect in the OFF position and tag it
  before you open the cabinet or reach in toward the shroud.
- The frame and the shroud are hot in normal running, and this code means they are
  getting hotter than usual. Let them cool first.
- Safety glasses on before the compressed air, not after.

## Do this now

1. Finish the piece you are on. This alert is about how fast the winding is
   heating, not how hot it is, so it usually arrives well before the motor is hot.
2. Open the dashboard and read both temperatures: the winding, from the
   thermocouple on the end turns, and the frame, from the NTC — Negative
   Temperature Coefficient — thermistor sitting in a cooling fin channel. Note the
   gap between them.
3. Ask honestly whether this work is heavier than a normal session — thicker
   stock, a slower feed, a blade that has been in service a while. If it is, the
   fast rise is legitimate.
4. At the next convenient break, lock out and look into the fan shroud on the back
   of the motor. If it is packed with sawdust, blow it out until you can see the
   fan blades and the cooling fins clearly.
5. Check the dust extraction that was supposed to keep it clear.
6. Restore the disconnect, press Start, and watch whether the alert returns on
   comparable work.

## Why it happens

The heat a given cut puts into the winding has not changed. What changed is how
fast the motor can get that heat back out.

This motor is a TEFC — Totally Enclosed Fan-Cooled. Sealed against sawdust, so it
cannot vent heat; everything it sheds leaves through the finned frame, pushed by
the shaft-driven fan behind the shroud. Service factor 1.0 leaves no margin to
soak up the difference. Take airflow away and the same cut drives the winding up a
steeper line — and that line is what this alert measures.

**A shroud packing up with sawdust is the most likely cause.** It is the
documented root cause of the original protector's failure on this saw, and it
comes back as fast as the dust extraction lets it.

The alert is not always right. Two things raise the rate of rise legitimately:

- **A genuinely heavier cut.** Thick hardwood, a slow feed, back-to-back rips with
  no idle time between them. The baseline came from one commissioning session under
  load — the comparison is only fair against work like that session's.
- **A dull or resin-loaded blade.** The motor really is working harder than the cut
  looks.

The signal that separates a dirty motor from a hard cut is the **frame-to-winding
delta**, and both numbers are on the status page. A heavier cut usually lifts them
together. Restricted airflow lifts the winding faster than the frame, so the gap
between the two widens — that widening gap is the airflow-restriction fingerprint,
and it is why this saw has two sensors instead of one.

So trust this alert most when it appears during work comparable to the baseline
session, and when it appears more often across successive sessions. One alert
during the heaviest rip of the day is weak evidence. The same alert on the cuts
you were making last month is strong evidence.

## When it clears

By itself. The slope is recomputed continuously over the trailing 60 seconds, so
the alert falls away as soon as the winding stops climbing steeply — the end of a
cut, a few idle minutes, or a cleaned shroud will all do it.

Nothing about starting the saw changed. The state never left `ARMED` and the relay
never opened, so there is nothing to reset. If you stopped the saw yourself to
clean the shroud, you press Start when you are done — the saw has no way to start
itself. The starter is a 3-wire momentary-Start / maintained-Stop circuit with a
seal-in auxiliary contact: break the coil circuit and the contactor drops and stays
dropped until a human presses the physical Start button. That is a property of the
wiring (`SR-4`), not of the firmware.

You can acknowledge the alert on the dashboard, or with a short press of the ack
button. That clears the message off the display and does nothing else.

## If it keeps happening

Appearing more often, session after session, on the same kind of work is the
result this system was built to produce. It means the shroud is filling faster
than your dust extraction can prevent. Once you know roughly how many sessions
that takes, clean it on a schedule and get ahead of the alert.

- **Interlock the dust collector with the saw** if you can. A collector that has to
  be switched on by hand gets forgotten, and this is the root cause rather than a
  symptom of it.
- **Check the blade and the belt.** A dull or pitchy blade loads the motor far past
  what the cut suggests, and a belt tensioned too tight adds mechanical drag all
  session long.
- **If the frame number barely moves** through a whole session while the winding
  climbs normally, suspect the NTC or its calibration rather than the motor. An
  NTC reading low quietly defeats this warning. Builder work.
- **If this alert never appears** but the saw keeps reaching [W01](W01.md) at
  95 °C and tripping at [E01](E01.md), the baseline was most likely captured
  during an unrepresentative session and sits too high to be useful.
  Re-establishing it is commissioning step 10 — a builder task.

Do not raise the 1.5x multiplier, and do not re-baseline against a dirty motor to
make this quiet. A baseline captured with a packed shroud makes the packed shroud
normal. This alert can reach you long before the winding is anywhere near 95 °C,
while stopping is still your choice.
