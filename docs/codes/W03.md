## What happened

At boot the supervisor recorded the winding temperature as this session's
baseline. It has now spent more than `DETACH_ALERT_MIN` (30 minutes) in `ARMED`
without once seeing that reading rise `DETACH_MIN_RISE_C` (5 °C) above the
baseline.

Nothing tripped and nothing is holding the saw. The relay is closed, the state is
`ARMED`, and the LED is solid — there is no LED pattern for this, only a dashboard
alert.

Most of the time this means exactly what it sounds like: the saw was on at the
wall and nobody cut anything.

## Do this now

1. Make one real cut in normal stock. Not a tap on a scrap — the motor has to do
   some work.
2. Watch the winding temperature on the dashboard while you cut and for a minute
   after.
3. If it climbs 5 °C above where the session started, the probe is verified. The
   alert clears itself and stays cleared.
4. If it does not move at all through genuine cutting, stop and get the builder.
   That is the case this alert exists to find.
5. Do not open the motor or the starter enclosure to inspect the probe yourself.
   Everything this alert points at sits at 240 V or inside the motor.

## Why it happens

The thermocouple is bonded to the winding end turns through an AlN — aluminium
nitride — substrate, which carries heat across while keeping the probe electrically
clear of a 230 V winding. If that bond works loose and the probe ends up dangling
near the winding instead of touching it, it keeps reporting calm, plausible,
ambient-ish numbers indefinitely. Every check in the protection loop passes: the
reading is in range, it is fresh, it carries no open-circuit or short flag, and it
never jumps. [E02](E02.md) and [E03](E03.md) catch a probe that is broken or
silent. Nothing catches a probe that is merely no longer touching anything.

The only tell is behavioural. A probe genuinely bonded to the end turns of a
working motor always warms up eventually. One that never warms is suspect.

Causes, most likely first:

- **The saw was armed and idle.** The supervisor is powered from a tap downstream
  of the machine disconnect, so it is awake and counting `ARMED` minutes whenever
  the machine is on at the wall — Start button or not. Half an hour of setup,
  measuring and coffee raises this alert on a perfectly healthy probe.
- **Only very light work so far.** A few short cuts in thin stock may not put 5 °C
  into a 3 HP motor.
- **The probe has come off the winding.** This is a belt-driven, cabinet-mounted
  saw and the motor shakes every time it starts. The bond at the end turns and the
  lead dressing are all that hold the sensor in place.

## When it clears

The moment the winding reads 5 °C above the session baseline. The verification
flag latches true there and never reverts, so one good cut settles it for the rest
of the session. A power cycle takes a fresh baseline and starts the check over.

This is deliberately soft. It is an alert, not a trip — a hard stop on "no rise
seen yet" would nuisance-stop everyone who armed the saw and walked away, which is
the most common reason it fires.

Nothing about starting the saw changed, because the relay never opened. If you did
stop the saw, it does not restart on its own: the starter is a 3-wire
momentary-Start / maintained-Stop circuit with a seal-in auxiliary contact, so a
human presses the physical Start button. That is the wiring (`SR-4`), not the
firmware.

A short press of the ack button clears the message off the dashboard. It does not
verify the probe.

## If it keeps happening

If the winding temperature does not move during genuine cutting, the probe is off
the winding. Treat that as real.

The supervisor is then blind: it will hold the relay closed, report comfortable
numbers, and never trip.

Under `SR-3` the passive bimetallic thermostat is the primary protection and is
independent of the ESP32 — but **confirm it is actually fitted before you rely on
it.** It is recorded as not yet ordered in
[ARCHITECTURE.md § Open questions](https://github.com/6ilo/tablesaw-thermal-protection/blob/main/ARCHITECTURE.md#open-questions).
If it is not in the coil circuit, a probe that has come off the winding leaves
this motor with no working thermal protection at all.

Even when fitted, the thermostat is the layer that acts last: no warning, no log,
no [W01](W01.md) at 95 °C, and the winding runs hotter than `TRIP_THRESHOLD`
(110 °C) before it opens.

That is a builder task, and not a small one: the motor end bell has to come off to
re-bond the sensor and re-dress the leads. Do not run heavy sessions in the
meantime.

Rule this out before anyone opens the motor. The baseline is captured at boot,
whatever the motor's temperature happens to be. If the supervisor power-cycles
while the motor is already warm — after a trip, or a disconnect flipped
mid-session — the baseline is that warm reading, and verification then needs 5 °C
on top of it. That is the most likely explanation for this alert
appearing after a hot restart on a saw whose probe is fine.

Acknowledging this alert clears the message, not the doubt. If the number never
moves while you are cutting, it is decoration.
