% ============================================================
% ladder_coil_circuit.tex — coil circuit after the retrofit.
%
% As an electrician sees it:
%
%   L1 -> RX -> STOP -> [START || M1 aux] -> OL -> M1 -> L2
%
% RX is the VONVOFF B07CTL3TG6 receiver, programmed to MOMENTARY
% mode, held closed by the ESP32's 433 MHz heartbeat.
%
% Terminal names and internal topology come from the manufacturer's
% own wiring diagram in the listing gallery:
%
%  1. It is LINE-POWERED and its output is NOT a dry contact. Four
%     screw terminals in two pairs — AC IN L/N and AC OUT L/N. The
%     relay switches L only, so AC OUT L is simply switched AC IN L,
%     and the two N terminals are a bonded common bus.
%
%  2. AC IN L must therefore stay permanently live, which puts the
%     receiver at the HEAD of the rung rather than between the
%     seal-in and the coil. Feed AC IN L from downstream of the
%     seal-in and the unit is unpowered — relay open — at the moment
%     START is pressed, so the coil never latches.
%
% The manufacturer diagram wires AC OUT L/N straight to a contactor
% coil (A1/A2). Do NOT copy that here: it bypasses the seal-in, so
% the saw would restart by itself when the link recovers. SR-4.
%
% There is no TSTAT on this sheet. The bimetallic thermostat is
% TASK-6 in ARCHITECTURE.md and has not been purchased.
%
% Regenerate with:  make ladder_coil_circuit.svg
% ============================================================

\def\cgap{0.16}
\def\cbar{0.34}

% \noc{<cx>}{<cy>}  — normally-open contact   -| |-
\newcommand{\noc}[2]{%
  \draw[wire] (#1-\cgap,#2-\cbar) -- (#1-\cgap,#2+\cbar);
  \draw[wire] (#1+\cgap,#2-\cbar) -- (#1+\cgap,#2+\cbar);
}
% \ncc{<cx>}{<cy>}  — normally-closed contact -|/|-
\newcommand{\ncc}[2]{%
  \noc{#1}{#2}%
  \draw[wire] (#1-0.34,#2-0.46) -- (#1+0.34,#2+0.46);
}
% \coilsym{<cx>}{<cy>} — output coil  -( )-
\newcommand{\coilsym}[2]{%
  \draw[wire] (#1,#2) circle (0.36);
}
% \dlabel{<cx>}{<name>}{<detail>} — name above, detail below
\newcommand{\dlabel}[3]{%
  \node[font=\small\bfseries, tsink, anchor=south] at (#1,0.86) {#2};
  \node[note, anchor=north, align=center] at (#1,-0.86) {#3};
}

\node[legendbox, anchor=north west, text width=10.4cm] at (0,-5.8) {%
  \textbf{How this receiver is actually built}\\[4pt]
  From the manufacturer's wiring diagram in the listing gallery. Four
  screw terminals in two pairs:\\[4pt]
  \begin{tabular}{@{}p{2.6cm}@{\hspace{5pt}}p{6.7cm}@{}}
    \texttt{AC IN L / N}  & the receiver's own supply. It runs off this
                            pair, so it has to stay energised.\\[3pt]
    \texttt{AC OUT L}     & \emph{switched} \texttt{AC IN L}. The relay
                            makes and breaks L, and this is
                            \textbf{not a dry contact}.\\[3pt]
    \texttt{AC OUT N}     & bonded to \texttt{AC IN N} --- a common
                            neutral bus, not a second pole. The
                            manufacturer's diagram lands the incoming
                            return here.\\
  \end{tabular}\\[5pt]
  So there is nothing to relocate and no jumper to fit: one L pair
  carries the rung, the N pair carries the return. Do not move the
  receiver between the seal-in and the coil --- that position assumes
  the dry relay module of TASK-3, which the project does not own.
};

\node[legendbox, anchor=north west, text width=10.4cm] at (11.0,-5.8) {%
  \textcolor{tsmains}{\textbf{Do not follow the manufacturer's diagram
  literally}}\\[4pt]
  That diagram runs \texttt{AC OUT L} and \texttt{AC OUT N} straight to
  a contactor's coil terminals \texttt{A1}/\texttt{A2}. For a pump or a
  dust collector that is fine and it is exactly the right idea --- let
  the little relay switch a coil, not the load.\\[5pt]
  \textbf{On a saw it is unsafe.} Wiring the coil directly bypasses the
  3-wire seal-in, so the coil is energised whenever the relay is closed
  and \textbf{the saw restarts by itself} the moment the RF link
  recovers or the motor cools. That is precisely what SR-4
  forbids.\\[5pt]
  This sheet keeps the seal-in. \texttt{AC OUT L} feeds \texttt{STOP},
  and after any trip the coil stays dropped until a human presses
  \texttt{START}.
};

\node[legendbox, anchor=north west, text width=10.4cm] at (0,-11.6) {%
  \textbf{Programming MOMENTARY --- the one step that matters}\\[4pt]
  Get this wrong and the system is fail-\emph{dangerous}: a latched
  relay holds its last state when the link dies.\\[4pt]
  \begin{tabular}{@{}p{1.6cm}@{\hspace{5pt}}p{7.7cm}@{}}
    8 presses & clears every paired code --- start here\\[2pt]
    \textbf{1 press} & \textbf{point movement (momentary)} --- required\\[2pt]
    2 presses & self-locking (toggle) --- fail-dangerous\\[2pt]
    3 presses & interlock --- fail-dangerous, and the factory default\\
  \end{tabular}\\[5pt]
  Then pair a fob, \textbf{hold} its ON button, and confirm the contact
  drops within about a second of release. Time that decay with a
  stopwatch and record it --- it is the budget the heartbeat has to
  beat, and § 7 step 1 tests against it.\\[5pt]
  \textcolor{tsmains}{\textbf{The listing's spec table says
  ``Contact Type: Normally Closed.''}} Do not trust that either way ---
  settle it by observation in the same step. If the contact is closed
  while nothing is transmitting, stop: the inversion this entire design
  rests on is not there.
};

\node[legendbox, anchor=north west, text width=10.4cm] at (11.0,-11.6) {%
  \textbf{What opens the rung}\\[4pt]
  Every element is either \emph{NC that opens on fault} or \emph{NO
  that must be actively held closed}. No failure leaves the coil
  energised.\\[5pt]
  \begin{tabular}{@{}p{1.35cm}@{\hspace{5pt}}p{1.15cm}@{\hspace{5pt}}p{5.9cm}@{}}
    \textbf{Element} & \textbf{Type} & \textbf{Opens on}\\[2pt]
    RX     & NO & loss of the 433\,MHz carrier --- ESP32 power loss,
                  watchdog reset, NTC fault, overtemp, dead fob cell,
                  jamming, or GPIO26 floating\\[1pt]
    STOP   & NC & operator command; a broken wire also opens\\[1pt]
    START  & NO & --- (must be held until M1 aux seals in)\\[1pt]
    M1 aux & NO & loss of coil power breaks the seal\\[1pt]
    OL     & NC & sustained overcurrent\\
  \end{tabular}
};

\node[legendbox, anchor=north west, text width=10.4cm] at (0,-17.8) {%
  \textbf{What this rung does not have yet}\\[4pt]
  \textcolor{tsmains}{\textbf{No passive layer.}} SR-3 is unmet. Until
  the bimetallic thermostat of TASK-6 is fitted in the ghost slot
  above, every protective function on this rung depends on the ESP32
  continuing to transmit.\\[4pt]
  \textcolor{tsmains}{\textbf{Frame, not winding.}} The DROK NTC reads
  the motor frame. It trails winding temperature, so thresholds are
  set from an observed baseline (§ 7 steps 8--9), not from the
  110\,\textdegree C figure used in ARCHITECTURE.md.\\[4pt]
  \textcolor{tssafe}{\textbf{SR-4 is met.}} Once the coil drops the
  seal-in breaks, so a human presses START. The receiver re-closing
  on its own never restarts the saw.
};
